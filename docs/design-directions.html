Part two

Not green

Four other worlds, built to the same rules: light quiet ground, one accent doing all the talking, greys tinted toward that accent so the palette reads as one decision rather than a colour dropped on top of grey.

Part four

Typography

Independent of the colour — any pairing works with any direction. Shown in neutral grey so the letterforms are the only thing you are judging. Body text and the mono used for labels stay the same in all three; only the display face changes.

A · Editorial

Anastasiia Horbachova

A serif with a real italic, used only for your name and section titles. This is the elegance you liked in the Tomas site — but ours leans quieter, without his red.

DisplayInstrument Serif
BodyInstrument Sans
LabelsJetBrains Mono

Warmest and most human. Slight risk: serifs are having a moment, so it is the least surprising of the three.

B · Structural

Anastasiia Horbachova

A tight, confident grotesque set heavy and large. No decoration anywhere — the impact comes purely from weight and spacing. The most “engineer” of the three.

DisplayArchivo
BodyInter Tight
LabelsJetBrains Mono

Cleanest and most neutral. Ages well, reads as precise, but carries the least personality on its own.

C · Distinctive

Anastasiia Horbachova

A variable serif with softened corners and deliberately odd letterforms. Nobody else’s portfolio is set in this. Memorable — and the one a stranger is most likely to comment on.

DisplayFraunces
BodyInter Tight
LabelsJetBrains Mono

The boldest call. Highest ceiling, and the one most dependent on getting the spacing exactly right.

What I need from you

One number and one letter. If nothing here is right, say what is wrong with the closest one — “too warm”, “too dark”, “the green is too grey” — and I will adjust rather than start over.
